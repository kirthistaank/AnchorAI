## 1. COGNITIVE DISTORTIONS (40 types)

Each distortion has: Definition | Example | Manifestation

### Thinking Errors & Absolutes

1. **All-or-Nothing Thinking**
   - Definition: Viewing situations in black/white categories with no middle ground
   - Example: "I made one mistake on the presentation, so I'm a total failure"
   - Manifestation: Perfectionism, harsh self-judgment

2. **Overgeneralization**
   - Definition: Taking one negative event and treating it as never-ending pattern
   - Example: "I bombed that interview, I'll never get a job"
   - Manifestation: Hopelessness, giving up

3. **Mental Filter**
   - Definition: Filtering out all positive evidence and focusing only on negatives
   - Example: Got 19/20 on test but only thinks about the one wrong answer
   - Manifestation: Low self-esteem, missing positive feedback

4. **Discounting the Positive**
   - Definition: Dismissing positive experiences as exceptions or flukes
   - Example: "They only complimented my work because they felt sorry for me"
   - Manifestation: Imposter syndrome, not internalizing success

5. **Jumping to Conclusions**
   - Definition: Making conclusions without sufficient evidence
   - Sub-type A: Mind Reading - "They think I'm stupid"
   - Sub-type B: Fortune Telling - "This will be a disaster"
   - Manifestation: Anxiety, social withdrawal

### Emotional Reasoning & Blame

6. **Emotional Reasoning**
   - Definition: Treating feelings as facts
   - Example: "I feel like a failure, therefore I am a failure"
   - Manifestation: Low mood, paralysis

7. **Should Statements**
   - Definition: Using rigid rules ("should," "must," "ought") about self and others
   - Example: "I should be able to work 12 hours a day without getting tired"
   - Manifestation: Guilt, resentment, burnout

8. **Personalization**
   - Definition: Assuming you caused events outside your control or taking criticism too personally
   - Example: "My friend didn't text back, it must be something I did"
   - Manifestation: Guilt, shame, anxiety

9. **Blame**
   - Definition: Blaming self for things outside control, or blaming others for your feelings
   - Example: "It's all my fault the project failed" (when team decision)
   - Manifestation: Guilt, resentment, victim mentality

### Catastrophizing & Worst-Case Thinking

10. **Catastrophizing**
    - Definition: Assuming the worst will happen or that current situation is unbearable
    - Example: "My boss didn't smile at me, I'm getting fired"
    - Manifestation: Anxiety, panic, hypervigilance

11. **Magnification/Minimization**
    - Definition: Exaggerating importance of negatives or shrinking importance of positives
    - Example: Small mistake = "disaster"; big achievement = "no big deal"
    - Manifestation: Anxiety, low mood

12. **Awfulizing**
    - Definition: Viewing situations as terrible or awful beyond actual severity
    - Example: "This is the worst thing that could ever happen"
    - Manifestation: Despair, overwhelm

13. **What-Ifs**
    - Definition: Endless chain of anxious "what if" questions
    - Example: "What if I have a panic attack? What if I can't handle it?"
    - Manifestation: Anxiety loops, avoidance

### Identity & Self-Concept Distortions

14. **Labeling**
    - Definition: Using single negative characteristic to define entire identity
    - Example: Made a social mistake → "I'm socially awkward" (identity)
    - Manifestation: Low self-esteem, avoidance

15. **Negative Self-Label**
    - Definition: Using harsh labels for yourself
    - Example: "I'm stupid," "I'm a loser," "I'm broken"
    - Manifestation: Depression, shame

16. **Comparison**
    - Definition: Comparing yourself unfavorably to others (often based on their highlight reel)
    - Example: "Everyone else has their life together, I'm the only one struggling"
    - Manifestation: Inadequacy, envy, low mood

17. **Impostor Syndrome**
    - Definition: Believing success is luck or deception, not deserved
    - Example: "I only got this job because they needed someone. I don't belong here."
    - Manifestation: Anxiety, imposter feelings despite evidence of competence

### Control & Responsibility Distortions

18. **False Responsibility**
    - Definition: Assuming responsibility for others' feelings or outcomes
    - Example: "My parent is sad, it's my job to fix it"
    - Manifestation: Guilt, anxiety, over-functioning

19. **Helplessness/Hopelessness**
    - Definition: Believing you have no control and nothing will change
    - Example: "There's no point trying, it won't matter"
    - Manifestation: Depression, giving up, learned helplessness

20. **Fortune Telling (Negative)**
    - Definition: Predicting the future will be negative with certainty
    - Example: "I'll never be happy," "This will always be this way"
    - Manifestation: Hopelessness, avoidance, depression

### Perception & Interpretation Distortions

21. **Selective Attention**
    - Definition: Only noticing information that confirms negative beliefs
    - Example: Ignoring all the times someone was kind, only remembering when they were critical
    - Manifestation: Relationship problems, low mood

22. **Misinterpretation**
    - Definition: Interpreting neutral events as negative
    - Example: Someone quiet during conversation = "They don't like me"
    - Manifestation: Social anxiety, relationship conflict

23. **Negative Prediction**
    - Definition: Assuming negative outcome before trying
    - Example: "I won't get the job, so why apply?"
    - Manifestation: Avoidance, missed opportunities

24. **Can't-Stand-It-Itis**
    - Definition: Believing you can't tolerate discomfort or difficult situations
    - Example: "I can't handle this anxiety," "I can't stand being alone"
    - Manifestation: Avoidance, anxiety, panic

### Relationship & Social Distortions

25. **Rejection Sensitivity**
    - Definition: Expecting rejection and interpreting neutral social cues as rejection
    - Example: "They probably hate me," "No one wants to spend time with me"
    - Manifestation: Social withdrawal, anxiety, low self-worth

26. **Mind Reading**
    - Definition: Believing you know what others think without evidence
    - Example: "They think I'm boring," "Everyone judging me"
    - Manifestation: Social anxiety, self-consciousness

27. **Relationship Catastrophizing**
    - Definition: Assuming one conflict means the relationship will end
    - Example: Had disagreement with partner → "We're going to break up"
    - Manifestation: Relationship anxiety, hypervigilance

28. **Assumed Obligation**
    - Definition: Believing you must do things to maintain relationships
    - Example: "I have to say yes even though I'm exhausted"
    - Manifestation: Resentment, burnout, boundary issues

### Cognitive Avoidance & Thought Distortions

29. **Rumination**
    - Definition: Repetitive, circular thinking about problems without resolution
    - Example: Going over an embarrassing moment 100 times
    - Manifestation: Anxiety, depression, insomnia

30. **Worry**
    - Definition: Repetitive anxious thinking about future unknowns
    - Example: Endless "what-if" scenarios about tomorrow
    - Manifestation: Anxiety, sleep disruption

31. **Thought Suppression**
    - Definition: Trying hard to avoid certain thoughts, which paradoxically increases them
    - Example: "Don't think about the embarrassing thing"... (now you can't stop)
    - Manifestation: Intrusive thoughts, anxiety

32. **Analysis Paralysis**
    - Definition: Over-thinking situations to the point of inability to decide/act
    - Example: Spending weeks researching decision instead of deciding
    - Manifestation: Procrastination, missed opportunities

### Values & Authenticity Distortions

33. **Shoulds** (variant)
    - Definition: Living by others' values instead of your own
    - Example: "I should want to be a lawyer" (but don't)
    - Manifestation: Resentment, depression, inauthenticity

34. **Perfectionism**
    - Definition: Setting unrealistic standards and treating anything less as failure
    - Example: One typo in email = "I'm not good at writing"
    - Manifestation: Anxiety, procrastination, burnout

35. **Over-Responsibility**
    - Definition: Taking on too much responsibility for others
    - Example: Fixing everyone's problems at work
    - Manifestation: Exhaustion, resentment, boundary issues

### Miscellaneous High-Impact Distortions

36. **Sunk Cost Fallacy**
    - Definition: Continuing something because you've invested time/effort, not because it's good
    - Example: "I've spent 5 years on this career, I can't change now"
    - Manifestation: Stuck in unhappy situations, resentment

37. **Availability Heuristic**
    - Definition: Overestimating how often bad things happen because you remember them easily
    - Example: Heard of one plane crash, now thinks flying is super dangerous
    - Manifestation: Anxiety, avoidance

38. **Black-and-White Morality**
    - Definition: Seeing people/situations as purely good or purely evil
    - Example: "If I'm not perfect, I'm bad. If they disagree, they're against me."
    - Manifestation: Shame, relationship conflict, rigidity

39. **Toxic Positivity**
    - Definition: Refusing to acknowledge negative feelings or reality
    - Example: "I should just be happy," denying real problems
    - Manifestation: Emotional suppression, unprocessed feelings

40. **Victim Mentality**
    - Definition: Viewing self as perpetual victim without agency
    - Example: "Everything bad happens to me, I have no control"
    - Manifestation: Helplessness, depression, missed agency

## 3. CBT THOUGHT RECORD STRUCTURE

The classic CBT tool for analyzing and reframing thoughts. Each entry captures:

```
SITUATION
├─ What happened? When? Where? Who?
└─ Example: "My boss didn't reply to my email for 2 hours"

AUTOMATIC THOUGHT(S)
├─ What immediately came to mind?
├─ Rate belief strength: 0-100%
└─ Examples: "I'm going to get fired", "I'm incompetent"

EMOTION(S)
├─ What did you feel?
├─ Rate intensity: 0-100%
└─ Examples: Anxiety (85%), Fear (70%), Self-doubt (60%)

BEHAVIOR(S)
├─ What did you do?
├─ Helpful or unhelpful?
└─ Examples: Refreshed email 20 times, couldn't focus on work

CONSEQUENCE
├─ What happened as a result?
└─ Examples: Wasted 2 hours, increased anxiety, no actual problem

EVIDENCE FOR (the automatic thought)
├─ Any facts supporting this thought?
└─ Examples: Boss didn't respond (but that's just a delay, not evidence of firing)

EVIDENCE AGAINST (the automatic thought)
├─ Facts that contradict the thought?
└─ Examples: 
│   - Boss has never fired anyone for this
│   - Boss delayed before but always replied
│   - I've been praised in reviews
│   - People get busy; doesn't mean firing

ALTERNATIVE THOUGHT (more balanced)
├─ What's a more realistic interpretation?
├─ Belief strength: 0-100%
└─ Example: "Boss is probably in meetings. Email delays don't mean job threat. I have 3 years of strong performance."

NEW EMOTION(S)
├─ How do you feel about the alternative thought?
└─ Examples: Concern (30%, manageable), Relief (70%)

ACTION PLAN
├─ What will you do differently next time?
└─ Example: 
    - Wait 4 hours before assuming
    - Focus on current task instead of email refresh
    - Remember past examples of boss replying
    - If still worried after 4 hours, send brief follow-up
```

## 4. COPING STRATEGIES (60+)

**General Strategies:**
1. Go for a walk
2. Exercise or workout (cardio, strength, yoga)
3. Dance to music
4. Stretch or do gentle movement

**⭐ Your Personal Strategies:**
- Mini walk in your backyard (5-20 minutes) - helps clear your mind while staying connected to your space
- 30-minute yoga session - gentle, grounding, helps regulate emotions
- Gardening activity - watering plants, tending to them - connects you to growth and nature

---

### Behavioral Coping Strategies - Connection & Gratitude

**General Strategies:**
5. Call a friend or family member
6. Meet someone in person
7. Join a group activity or class
8. Volunteer or help someone
9. Spend time with a pet

**⭐ Your Personal Strategies:**
- Talk to your supportive husband - lean on his presence and emotional support

### Grounding Techniques

17. 5-4-3-2-1 sensory technique (notice 5 things you see, 4 hear, etc.)
18. Hold ice cubes in your hands
19. Splash cold water on face
20. Notice textures around you
21. Focus on breath (slow, intentional breathing)

### Cognitive Coping Strategies - Thought Challenging

**General Strategies:**
32. Write down and examine your automatic thoughts
33. Use Thought Record technique

### Cognitive Coping Strategies - Reframing

**General Strategies:**
36. Look for the helpful aspects of a difficult situation
37. Ask "What would I tell a friend in this situation?"
38. Consider the best, worst, and most realistic outcome
39. Practice gratitude (what am I grateful for?)
40. Find humor in the situation (if appropriate)

---

### Cognitive Coping Strategies - Reflection & Positive Focus

**General Strategies:**
41. Think about past successes
42. Remind yourself of your values and strengths
43. Reflect on how previous difficult situations resolved

**⭐ Your Personal Strategy:**
- Reflect on positive outcomes - reviewing how past struggles turned out okay, building confidence and perspective
- Be grateful for what you have - consciously acknowledge the good things in your life (supportive husband, beautiful backyard, time for self-care)

---

### Cognitive Coping Strategies - Attention Shifting

44. Focus on your current activity fully (mindfulness)
45. Notice positive things around you
46. Watch something engaging (movie, video, educational content)

**⭐ Your Personal Strategy:**
- Watch YouTube videos from your favorite speakers - absorbs your attention + provides inspiration and encouragement

---

### Cognitive Coping Strategies - Self-Talk & Encouragement

**General Strategies:**
47. Use compassionate self-talk ("I'm doing my best")
48. Remind yourself: "This is temporary"
49. Use affirmations ("I am capable")
50. Talk to yourself like a good friend would

**⭐ Your Personal Strategy:**
- Give yourself encouraging words - speak to yourself with kindness and affirmation, as your husband does for you

---

### Emotion Regulation (DBT-inspired)

51. Allow yourself to feel the emotion (don't resist)
52. Name the emotion specifically
53. Remind yourself emotions are temporary
54. Use opposite action (if sad, move body in energetic way)
55. Practice self-compassion

---

### Acceptance & Mindfulness

**General Strategies:**
56. Meditate (guided or silent)
57. Practice body scan meditation
58. Accept the thought without fighting it
59. Practice mindful breathing
60. Journal about your feelings

**⭐ Your Personal Strategy:**
- Gentle reflection and mindfulness in your backyard - sit with your feelings, watch nature, breathe
